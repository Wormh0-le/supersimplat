# Reconcile gsplat validity-boundary contributor flips against the RGB rasterization

The locked gsplat revision evaluates the shared per-Gaussian alpha in two separate CUDA translation units: the RGB rasterization kernel and the complete contributor-ID kernels. Their compiled float32 arithmetic disagrees by a few ulps, which is immaterial except exactly at the kernels' shared decision boundaries — the `1/255` validity cut, the `1e-4` transmittance termination cut, and sigma zero — where one kernel can accept a contributor the other rejected. Measured on the frozen controlled-overlap fixture at the locked 1008-pixel Anchor, exactly one of 1,016,064 pixels differed: the contributor stream accepted a Gaussian whose exact alpha is `0.003921566848368366`, `1.78e-9` below the validity cut, while the RGB kernel excluded it, producing a `0.0012227` mass mismatch (`rendererMassMismatch`) that blocked the production trial. The contributor kernels, not the RGB kernel, made the boundary error; the float64 reference chain reproduces the RGB alpha to `3.3e-8`.

The production renderer reconciles only proven boundary flips and fails closed on anything else. When a pixel's contributor mass exceeds the unchanged `2e-6 + 1e-5 * abs(alpha)` tolerance, the backend replays that pixel's tile from the same projection/tile preparation. The reference follows the source expression grouping with an explicit binary32 rounding after every scalar add, subtract, and multiply; alpha clamping and transmittance are also binary32. Python cannot invoke the locked CUDA translation unit's `__expf` intrinsic or reproduce its compiler contraction bit for bit, so it uses a correctly rounded binary32 exponential only to enumerate conservative candidate decisions. An intrinsic-specific disagreement that lacks a unique boundary explanation therefore fails closed rather than being treated as an exact replay.

A contributor is ambiguous only when the high-precision diagnostic evaluation sits within float32 evaluation noise of a boundary — `1e-8` of the validity cut (about 21 float32 ulps; the measured flip was `1.78e-9`, and `__expf`'s documented worst case at the cut's sigma of ~5.5 is ~8 ulps, plus float32 sigma-evaluation noise), `1e-9` of the termination cut, or `1e-9` of sigma zero — and at most four ambiguous contributors per pixel are enumerated. A genuine flip beyond the validity window fails closed rather than widening the class of defects the reconciler can mask. The repair is confined to a proven flip on an otherwise sound stream: the kernel stream's weights must be finite and must already reproduce the contributor kernels' own alpha within mass tolerance; exactly one replayed decision variant must reproduce the RGB alpha within `1e-6` and differ from exactly one replayed kernel-ID variant; and every weight in that kernel decision chain must agree with the kernel output within `max(1e-8, 16 × chain-position float32 ulps)`. The position-scaled budget accounts for the preceding transmittance multiplications already contained in a later contributor weight; checking the complete chain still rejects balanced or otherwise hidden material defects.

At the termination cut, a second proven direction is necessary for dense real scenes: the RGB kernel can retain one boundary Gaussian with remaining transmittance just above the cut while the contributor kernel and scalar replay round the same update just below it. Reconciliation accepts this direction only when there is exactly one termination ambiguity, the excluded contributor prefix matches its scalar replay, the RGB remaining transmittance is above the cut by at most two RGB-alpha ulps, the missing RGB mass matches that boundary Gaussian within the same proof budget, and the first later valid Gaussian would immediately terminate. These conditions prove that there is no unknowable tail; the single missing mass is assigned to the single boundary Gaussian. A below-cut RGB value, an above-cut successor, or any non-unique explanation still fails closed. The pixel's contributor IDs and weights are then rebuilt from the RGB-authoritative decision, so attribution stays tied to the same rasterization that produced service RGB and alpha.

The sealed Render Policy records `gsplat-boundary-contributor-reconciliation/v3`. This revision changes only the Companion's fail-closed proof, not the editor RGB appearance semantics, the unchanged `2e-6 + 1e-5 × abs(alpha)` mass tolerance, or the locked gsplat/CUDA identity. Any mismatch no boundary variant explains — a missing or extra non-boundary contributor, a divergent raster alpha, a non-finite or inconsistent kernel weight, any material chain-weight defect, an unflipped kernel stream, more than four candidates, or multiple matching variants — still raises `rendererMassMismatch`. The implementation neither truncates contributor support nor substitutes approximate attribution, and it changes no per-Gaussian evidence beyond the uniquely proven boundary mass. Avoiding the boundary through fixture or camera changes remains rejected because real captures can carry boundary Gaussians at any pixel.
